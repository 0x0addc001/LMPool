# LMPool Oral Script: 10-Minute Version

## Slide 1: Title (0:00-0:20)

Good morning. I am Jialiang Li. I will present LMPool, which
combines KV-aware routing with direct NVLink KV-cache transfer. The principle
is simple: route when possible, and transfer only when profitable.

Pointer: point first to the title, then to the repository URL.

## Slide 2: The Placement Mismatch (0:20-1:00)

Prefix caching assumes that a request runs where its KV already resides. But
data-parallel GPUs have independent allocators and caches. Round robin may
choose the wrong GPU, strict owner routing may overload one GPU, and
unconditional transfer may cost more than recomputation. LMPool coordinates
both placements.

Pointer: move from the two left boxes to the three bullets, then finish at the
design question.

## Slide 3: Two Principles (1:00-1:35)

The left principle is cache locality: route toward a useful contiguous prefix
while considering queue and capacity pressure. The right is cache fluidity:
copy or move a complete chain when future demand no longer matches placement.
Routing comes first because avoiding transfer is cheaper.

Pointer: alternate between the blue routing box and the green transfer box.

## Slide 4: System Architecture (1:35-2:15)

At the top, LLMEngine receives requests. The control plane stores versioned
global metadata. Every GPU owns a data plane with a Local Scheduler, Local
Block Manager, Model Runner, and physical KV cache. The control plane never
carries KV tensors. Model Runners exchange packed tensors over the green
NVLink path.

Pointer: trace one control arrow from LLMEngine to Global Scheduler, then a
metadata arrow to one worker, and finally the green NVLink arrow.

## Slide 5: A Request Through LMPool (2:15-3:00)

LLMEngine creates a Sequence and chained hashes for complete blocks. A new
ingress request has no local GPU, so the router evaluates every healthy rank.
The selected worker reuses ready blocks and allocates missing ones. A real
shortage may enter the transfer gate. Model Runner performs prefill and
decode. Only ready KV is published; reusable prefixes may remain cached after
completion.

Pointer: move down the numbered list and pause at Global route, Optional
foreground transfer, and Publication.

## Slide 6: KV-Aware Routing (3:00-3:50)

The formula estimates work on GPU \(g\). \(R\), \(N\), and \(S\) are request

The weights put these terms on one scale. I select minimum cost, not maximum

## Slide 7: Transactional KV Transfer (3:50-4:30)

Prepare locks the source and reserves the target. Execute transfers the packed
tensor. Publish registers ready destination blocks and makes them routable.
Finalize keeps the source for copy or safely releases it for move. Any earlier
failure aborts, preserves the source, and frees the reservation.

Pointer: follow Prepare, Execute, Publish, and Finalize on the lifecycle
figure; point to the abort path last.

## Slide 8: Cost-Gated Transfer Admission (4:30-5:25)

The left branch maps payload \(X\) to this pair's measured P95 curve, then
adds a manual 40-millisecond prior and a 1.2 interference factor. The right
branch learns nonnegative source and dispatch-to-publish residual EWMAs for
each pair and size bucket. Admission takes the maximum of static and observed
estimates. A plan proceeds only when saved prefill exceeds
\(\rho T_{\mathrm{xfer}}\), and source generation and target capacity still
match the plan.

Pointer: trace top, left and right branches, center maximum, and bottom
admit/reject diamond in that order.

## Slide 9: Evaluation Design (5:25-5:55)

I evaluate six RTX 3090 GPUs, two Qwen3 models, equal per-worker KV budgets,
five trials, and fixed 64-token outputs. Four workloads isolate routing, load
skew, memory pressure, and forecast-assisted handoff. These are internal
ablations, not a production comparison.

Pointer: point to the hardware bullets, then the workload table, then Scope.

## Slide 10: Routing Cuts Uncached Prefill (5:55-6:30)

Routing raises cached-token coverage from 44 to about 72 percent and halves
uncached prefill. Requests remain balanced. But 0.6B TTFT rises by 7.4
percent, while 1.7B TTFT falls by 10.2 percent. I claim reduced prefill work,
not universal latency improvement.

Pointer: compare the Round robin and KV-aware rows for each model, then point
to the final latency bullet.

## Slide 11: NVLink Data-Path Calibration (6:30-7:00)

The table covers every size and all six model-pair combinations. Bandwidth
rises from about 8 GiB per second at one block to 36.5 at 64 blocks because
larger plans split fixed data-path work/overhead. Every destination passes
byte-for-byte validation. This calibrates transfer, not serving benefit.

## Slide 12: Forecast-Assisted Session Handoff (7:00-7:40)

Warm-up creates 32 distinct long prefixes. Each group repeats shared text 16
times and adds a short suffix. One request per group runs on source ranks 0,
2, and 4. After all finish, the benchmark reveals exact demand for 96 reuse
requests. The right-side flow admits three plans with 224 blocks. Placement
leases then stripe requests across source and replica.

Pointer: point to Warm-up, then follow the right-side flow to Admit Copy Plan,
and return to Reuse phase and Scope.

## Slide 13: End-to-End Handoff Results (7:40-8:20)

Full LMPool improves throughput by 4.4 to 7.4 percent. Mean TTFT is 32.4 to
40.4 percent lower, and E2E is 9.3 to 12.9 percent lower. Transfer-only reaches
the same cached-token coverage but less throughput. Routing must use the new
replica.

Pointer: compare Round robin, Transfer only, and LMPool vertically; then point
to the three bold summary ranges.

## Slide 14: Why Routing and Transfer Must Cooperate (8:20-8:50)

Round robin and transfer-only keep a 3.3 request ratio and about 31 to 35
percent GPU utilization. Full LMPool lowers the ratio to 1.8 and raises
utilization above 50 percent. The lease turns copied KV into parallel capacity.

Pointer: read across the Transfer only and Full LMPool rows.

## Slide 15: Where Transfer Does Not Help (8:50-9:15)

Transfer is not always useful. Memory pressure may provide no profitable
reusable plan. Under steady load skew, round robin naturally warms every GPU.
Rejecting movement in these cases is part of the policy.

Pointer: contrast the Memory skew and Steady load skew boxes.

## Slide 16: Limitations and Future Work (9:15-9:40)

Handoff uses exact future demand, not an online predictor. The baselines are
internal, the models are small, and block size is fixed. I have not measured
cost error on independent plans or sensitivity to the 40-millisecond prior.
The control plane can rebuild metadata, but has no replicated consensus or
launcher failover. These define the next work.

Pointer: group the first three bullets as evaluation scope, the next two as
cost-model evidence, and the last as availability.

## Slide 17: Takeaways (9:40-9:55)

Routing solves cache locality. Cost-gated NVLink transfer provides cache
fluidity. They help only when they cooperate and when future reuse repays the
full transaction. Route when possible, and transfer when profitable.

Pointer: point to each numbered takeaway, then finish at the green sentence.
